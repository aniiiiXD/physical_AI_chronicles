The actual gap

World models exist. None of them run on the robot.

Every serious world model lab — 1X, DeepMind, Google — is training at cloud scale. That is correct. But a world model only earns its name when the robot uses it to plan: encode the current observation, roll out N imagined futures conditioned on candidate actions, pick the best one, act. That loop needs to close in under 100ms on the hardware bolted to the robot's compute board.

Nobody has benchmarked this. Nobody has built the distillation and inference pipeline for it. The gap between "we trained a world model" and "the robot plans with it on-device" is a systems + compression problem — and it is completely open.

Your technical edge

World model engineering, not VLA wrapping.

These techniques require deep architectural understanding. They are the reason this work signals research-level competence, not engineering convenience.

01

RSSM implementation from scratch

The Recurrent State Space Model is the heart of DreamerV3: a stochastic RNN that learns a compact latent dynamics model of the world. Implementing it from scratch — encoder, prior, posterior, decoder, predictor — is how you prove you understand what a world model actually is.

02

Parallelized latent rollouts

The imagine() function unrolls the world model N steps into the future. Naïve sequential rollout is too slow for on-device planning. Batching candidate action sequences and running parallel rollouts — then selecting the best trajectory — is the key latency-hiding trick.

03

Action-conditional compression

Quantizing a dynamics model is different from quantizing a VLA — you must preserve the action-conditioning structure. INT8 quantization on the RSSM transition model, validated by checking that action-conditional rollout accuracy degrades less than 5% from fp16.

04

Latent state caching + warm starts

The world model's latent state from the previous timestep is the best prior for the next. Caching and warm-starting rollouts — rather than cold-starting from observation every tick — cuts the per-step cost dramatically. Classic systems optimization applied to a new architecture.

01

World Model Theory

the core — go deepest here
  • State-space modelsF

    The mathematical framework: latent state, transition, emission. RSSM is a stochastic SSM. Understand Kalman filters first — DreamerV3 is their learned, nonlinear descendant.

  • DreamerV3 end-to-endF

    Read the paper (Hafner 2023) and every predecessor (Dreamer, DreamerV2). Understand symlog, free bits, KL balancing, the world model / actor / critic split. This is your implementation target.

  • RSSM architectureC

    Deterministic + stochastic state, prior vs posterior, the ELBO loss. Implement the forward pass before you train anything — understand what each tensor is.

  • Planning in latent spaceC

    imagine() — the function that makes a world model useful for control. Latent rollouts, trajectory optimization, actor-critic in imagination. This is what robots actually use the model for.

  • JEPA and masked predictionC

    LeCun's Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture — the alternative paradigm. Predicts in representation space, not pixel space. Read V-JEPA. Understand why 1X might prefer this over RSSM.

  • Video as world model pretrainingE

    Why 1X hired a video model researcher. Passive video prediction learns physics implicitly — but robots need action-conditioning. The gap between the two is the open research problem.

  • Action-conditional predictionE

    Conditioning rollouts on proposed actions is what separates observation prediction from robot-useful planning. How to train, evaluate, and benchmark action-conditional accuracy.

02

Generative Modeling

the representation backbone
  • VAEs and latent dynamicsF

    The encoder-decoder architecture underlying DreamerV3's world model. Reparameterization trick, ELBO, posterior collapse. The RSSM posterior is a learned VAE posterior over world states.

  • Transformers — deepF

    Read the Attention paper and the annotated transformer. Then read IRIS to see how transformers replace the RSSM. You need to understand both architectures to benchmark them fairly.

  • Discrete tokenizationC

    IRIS uses VQ-VAE-style discrete tokens as world state representations. Discrete tokens enable transformer world models. Also the connection to FAST action tokenization in VLAs.

  • Diffusion fundamentalsC

    DDPM → DDIM → flow matching. Required to understand DIAMOND (diffusion world model) and π0's action head. Understand the inference-time compute tradeoff — important for on-device.

  • Representation quality metricsC

    PSNR, SSIM, FID for pixel predictions. Cosine similarity in latent space. Linear probe accuracy. What each metric actually tells you about whether the world model is learning the right things.

  • Contrastive + SSL methodsE

    BYOL, SimCLR, DINO — the self-supervised learning landscape behind JEPA-style models. Understanding why prediction in representation space might generalize better than pixel prediction.

03

Systems & On-Device Engineering

your differentiator — apply to a new domain
  • GPU execution modelF

    Warps, shared memory, roofline. RSSM inference is memory-bandwidth bound, not compute bound — knowing this tells you where quantization wins.

  • RNN inference optimizationC

    Sequential RNN steps don't parallelize across time. Techniques: parallel scan, selective state spaces (Mamba), and GRU kernel fusion. The bottleneck is different from transformer inference.

  • Quantization for dynamics modelsC

    INT8 on the transition model, fp16 on the stochastic sampler. Calibration is harder than for classifiers — the model accumulates error over rollout steps. Measure per-step vs accumulated error.

  • CUDA streams + batched rolloutsC

    Parallelizing N candidate action trajectories through the world model. Each trajectory is an independent rollout — batch them into one GPU call instead of running sequentially.

  • Jetson Orin deploymentC

    TensorRT on Orin, power budgeting, thermal constraints. The RSSM is an RNN — verify TensorRT handles recurrent state correctly. Measure latency per rollout step and total planning cycle time.

  • Latent state managementE

    Warm-starting rollouts from the previous timestep's latent state. Managing the recurrent state across planning calls without recomputing the full observation encoder. The key to sub-100ms planning.

  • Reproducible benchmarkingC

    Fixed seeds, warmup steps, statistical reporting, Docker pinning. Rigor here is why Artifact A gets cited instead of ignored.

04

Robotics & Embodied Context

speak the language of the labs you're targeting
  • Model-based RL foundationsF

    Dyna, MBPO, MuZero — the lineage DreamerV3 descends from. Understanding model-based vs model-free tells you why world models matter and what they buy over VLAs.

  • Open robotics datasetsF

    Open X-Embodiment (1M+ trajectories, 22 embodiments), BridgeData V2, DROID, LIBERO. Know how to load, preprocess, and split these. Your training data for Artifact A.

  • Sim environmentsC

    LIBERO, DMControl, MuJoCo, Isaac Lab. DMControl for initial DreamerV3 validation (published baselines to verify against). LIBERO for robotics generalization.

  • Evaluation methodologyC

    Task success rate, multi-step prediction accuracy, latent space probing. Understand what each measures and what it misses. Proposing a better eval metric for world models is a paper contribution by itself.

  • The VLA-to-world-model transitionC

    Read π0, RT-2, and DreamerV3 back to back. Understand exactly why VLAs are a local optimum and what world models fix. This is the argument you need to make clearly in any lab conversation.

  • 1X and OpenAI robotics research landscapeE

    Follow 1X's NEO demos, Sam Sinha's prior work at Luma, and OpenAI's physical AI direction. Know the specific problems each team is working on — not generally "robotics," specifically their open problems.
